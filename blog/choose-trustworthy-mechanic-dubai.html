How to Choose a Trustworthy Mechanic in Dubai: 7 Questions to Ask

May 2026 · By Rania Farouk
← Back to Blog

Dubai has hundreds of workshops — from single-bay operations in Al Quoz to multi-brand specialists in DIP. Finding one that's technically competent, honest about what your car needs and fair on pricing is genuinely difficult, especially when you're new to the city or don't have a trusted recommendation. These seven questions help you filter quickly.

Question 1

Can you show me the old parts after the job?

A trustworthy workshop has no problem showing you removed parts. If a workshop is reluctant to return old parts or show them to you, ask yourself why. Some parts genuinely can't be returned (fluids, filters mixed with waste oil) but brake pads, shock absorbers, belts and hard parts should always be available to inspect.

Question 2

Can you explain why this repair is needed — specifically?

A good mechanic can explain the failure clearly in plain terms. If the answer is vague ("it's just worn out," "it's old") without any specifics, that's a warning sign. Ask them to show you the problem on the car — a worn brake pad, a leaking seal, a cracked boot. Honest workshops welcome this transparency.

Question 3

Is this urgent or can it wait?

A trustworthy mechanic distinguishes between safety-critical items (brake wear, tyre damage, steering faults) and items that need attention at the next service (minor leaks, cosmetic wear). If every item is presented as equally urgent, be cautious. Good workshops help you prioritise.

Question 4

What's the written quote — parts and labour separated?

Always get a written quote with parts cost and labour cost itemised separately. This lets you cross-check the parts pricing against the market and understand what the labour rate is. Workshops that can only give a single "job total" number make it impossible to evaluate fairness.

Question 5

Are these OEM, OES or aftermarket parts — and why?

OEM (Original Equipment Manufacturer) parts are made by the same suppliers who supply the dealer. OES (Original Equipment Supplier) are identical parts without the brand box. Quality aftermarket is cheaper but varies widely. A transparent workshop explains which they're using and why — and lets you choose. A workshop that can't answer this or defaults to "same as dealer" without specifics is unclear about their parts sourcing.

Question 6

What warranty do you offer on this repair?

A confident workshop offers a warranty on parts and labour — typically 3–6 months or 5,000–10,000 km for most repairs. No warranty offered means no accountability if the repair fails immediately. This matters most for major jobs: clutch, brakes, suspension.

Question 7

Do you have experience with my specific make and model?

Modern cars require model-specific knowledge — particularly for electronics, software calibration after part replacement and engine management. Ask specifically: have you worked on this engine / this model before? A BMW N63 specialist and a general mechanic are not equivalent. Specialists in your brand will have the right diagnostic tools, the right technical bulletins and the experience to anticipate known issues.

Red Flags to Watch For

  • Pressure to approve multiple repairs immediately without time to consider
  • Refusal to provide a written quote before work starts
  • Vague diagnosis without showing you the problem
  • No physical address or trade licence visible
  • Reviews that all sound identical or appear in short bursts (fake reviews)
  • Significantly cheaper than all competitors — sustainable pricing is possible, but extreme undercutting often means compromised parts or shortcuts
