Fuel Types in Dubai: Do You Really Need Special 98 Petrol or Is Super 95 Fine?

November 2025 · By Karim Al-Rashidi
← Back to Blog

At UAE petrol stations you'll find three grades: Special 95 (or E-Plus), Super 98, and sometimes diesel. Many drivers fill with whatever costs less or is at the nearest pump without thinking about it. Here's when that matters — and when it doesn't.

What Octane Rating Actually Means

Octane rating measures a fuel's resistance to pre-ignition (knocking). Higher compression engines — particularly turbocharged and performance engines — require high-octane fuel to avoid knocking, which occurs when the air-fuel mixture ignites before the spark plug fires. Knock causes power loss, rough running, and over time can damage pistons and cylinder walls.

Lower compression, naturally aspirated engines don't produce enough cylinder pressure to cause knock on 95 octane fuel. For these engines, paying for 98 octane provides no measurable benefit.

Does Your Car Need 98?

Check your owner's manual — specifically the minimum fuel octane requirement, not the recommended grade. There are three scenarios:

  • "Minimum 95 octane": 95 is perfectly fine. 98 won't damage it, but won't measurably help either.
  • "Minimum 98 octane" or "Premium fuel required": Use 98. Running 95 long-term in a high-compression or turbocharged engine causes the ECU to retard ignition timing to prevent knock — reducing power and potentially increasing fuel consumption, negating any cost saving.
  • "98 recommended but 95 acceptable": 95 works but may slightly reduce peak power output in performance modes. For regular daily driving, the difference is minimal.

The Dubai Heat Factor

There is a heat-related argument for 98 octane in Dubai. Higher ambient temperatures mean higher intake air temperatures, which increases the tendency for pre-ignition. Some vehicles that run fine on 95 in cooler climates exhibit occasional knock on 95 during Dubai summers. If your car on 95 shows any hesitation or roughness in summer heat, switching to 98 is a reasonable experiment.

Does 98 Improve Fuel Economy?

For engines designed for 98: yes, marginally — the ECU can advance ignition timing for better efficiency. For engines designed for 95: no meaningful difference. The price premium for 98 is approximately 10–15% in the UAE. Any efficiency gain is unlikely to offset this cost for 95-spec engines.

Dubai Fuel Grades Quick Reference

  • Special 95 (E-Plus): Standard grade. Suitable for most regular passenger cars with NA engines under normal conditions.
  • Super 98: Premium grade. Required for high-compression, turbocharged and performance engines. Recommended for all vehicles in heavy summer heat as a precaution.
  • Diesel: For diesel engines only. Never put diesel in a petrol engine.

When in doubt, check the fuel cap sticker or owner's manual. If the label says "95 min" you're not gaining anything from 98. If it says "98 min" or "premium", use it.
